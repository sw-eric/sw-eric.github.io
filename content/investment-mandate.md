This document defines what Sonsu Research trades, why, and the hard rules that govern position sizing, risk, and review. Every trade memo will reference back to this document. 

## Objective

Build and document a value-oriented research and trading process focused on US and Asia-Pacific AI infrastructure, energy, and macro markets. The capital base of $1,000 is a learning vehicle; the deliverable is the process and the body of written work. **Profit is not the primary success metric.** Process quality, writeup quality, and what I learn from each cycle are what define success here.

## Niche

The investable focus is concentrated, not eclectic.

- **AI infrastructure and energy (US and Asia-Pacific), ~60-70% of book.** Power generation and distribution, data center operators, cooling and electrical equipment, semiconductor supply chain names with direct exposure to AI CapEx. Both US-listed (e.g., VST, CEG, GEV, DLR, EQIX, NVDA, ASML, AMAT, TSM) and APAC-listed via ADRs and ETFs where accessible (e.g., EWY, Asian utility and industrial exposure).
- **Asia-Pacific macro, ~20-30% of book.** APAC equity market themes, currency-related expressions where retail-accessible, Asian-listed names with US ADRs (e.g., CPNG), country and regional ETFs (EWY, EWT, EWJ).
- **Opportunistic, up to ~10% of book.** I will allow myself the freedom to take a small allocation in compelling situations outside the niche, on the condition that any such trade has a fully written pre-trade memo and an explicit acknowledgment of why it falls outside the mandate.

## Edge hypothesis

I claim three sources of differentiated insight. None of these is a permanent moat. All require continuous work to remain real.

1. **Linguistic and cultural fluency in Korean markets.** Direct access to Korean primary-source financial press and disclosures, real-time exposure to local sentiment, and an ability to evaluate Korean management commentary in the original language.
2. **Ground-truth exposure to semiconductor and infrastructure capex** via prior project work on ASML's Hwaseong R&D Center and broader capital projects advisory experience. I have seen how capex decisions get reviewed at the line-item level. This shapes how I read industry capex commentary.
3. **Cross-border perspective from a non-US upbringing** across Korea, Malaysia, and Bangladesh, supporting analysis of supply chain, policy, and consumption themes that span these economies.

## Investment style

Discretionary. Fundamental and value-oriented as the primary lens, with thematic and macro overlays. I look for businesses where my work supports a view that diverges from the consensus and where the market price offers a margin of safety relative to that view.

Holding periods range from one to twelve months. The portfolio is concentrated: typically three to five positions plus any active hedges. I prefer fewer, better-researched positions to a diversified book; with $1,000, broad diversification is meaningless and concentration is the only way to express a real view.

## Position sizing and risk limits

These are hard rules. Violating any of them triggers a process review before further trading.

- **Maximum single position size:** 30% of capital ($300) at entry. Positions may grow beyond this through appreciation; I will not add to a position above 30%.
- **Maximum sector concentration:** 60% of capital. AI infrastructure and energy are treated as one sector for this purpose.
- **Per-trade stop loss:** -25% from entry, OR explicit thesis invalidation, whichever comes first. Each pre-trade memo specifies the conditions under which the thesis is invalidated.
- **Portfolio drawdown trigger:** A peak-to-trough drawdown of -20% triggers a 30-day pause on new positions and a written review of what went wrong before resuming.
- **Cash floor:** A minimum of 10% in cash at all times as dry powder, except in transient periods immediately after closing a position.
- **No trade without a written pre-trade memo.** No exceptions. The memo can be short, but it must exist before the order goes in.

## Long-biased core with tactical hedges

The book is long-biased. Hedges are tactical, not structural. I hedge specific positions or thematic exposures when I have an articulable reason — an event risk, an asymmetric setup, a macro overhang — not because hedging feels prudent in general.

Hedging instruments are limited to inverse and broad-market ETFs (e.g., SH, PSQ, SQQQ used carefully) and equity put options on liquid underlyings.

## Options discipline

Options are permitted with the following constraints. I treat options as a learning instrument first and a hedging instrument second. The math on small-account hedging is unforgiving and I want to be honest about that on this site.

- **Maximum capital allocated to options at any time:** 5% of book ($50). This is a deliberately small budget. The point is to develop intuition for Greeks, decay, and structuring without options dominating the PnL.
- **Maximum simultaneous open option positions:** two.
- **Permitted strategies:** long puts (for protection or directional bearish bets), long calls (for directional bullish bets when the underlying view is high-conviction but the cash equity is too capital-intensive), and cash-secured puts (only when I would be a willing buyer of the underlying at the strike).
- **Prohibited strategies:** naked calls, ratio spreads, short straddles or strangles, and any strategy with theoretically unbounded loss.
- **Every option position requires a written memo** that explicitly addresses (a) what the position is expressing that the cash equivalent could not, (b) the breakeven, (c) the maximum loss, and (d) the exit plan.

## Universe constraints

- US-listed equities, ADRs, and ETFs only. No directly-held foreign stocks, no foreign-listed instruments.
- No futures. No FX. No leveraged ETFs (other than for tactical short-side hedging within the limits above). No crypto. No private securities.
- No margin beyond what is required to enable the option strategies above. Cash account behavior preferred.

## What this project is and is not

It is a documented research and trading process designed to build skills, produce written work, and demonstrate disciplined thinking. It is the place where I build the habits I want to bring into institutional roles.

It is not a real track record. The capital base is too small and the sample size will be too low to draw statistical inference about skill. Sharpe ratios and similar metrics will be reported, but treated as descriptive, not predictive. It is not investment advice for any other person. It is not a fund.

## Review and revision

The mandate is a living document. I do not commit to a fixed review cadence; instead, I update the mandate whenever I learn something material that should change how I trade. Every revision is preserved in the git history and announced in the next monthly journal entry. Hard rules (position sizing, drawdown trigger, options budget) are not changed mid-trade or in response to a single bad outcome.
