## Thesis

GE Vernova reported a quarter that raised nearly every guidance line, and the stock is still trading below where it was before the print.

Third quarter orders were \$29.3 billion, up 96% organically, with Power orders alone at \$21.8 billion ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001996810/000199681026000162/gevpressrelease3q26.htm)). Revenue was \$12.6 billion, up 27%, and Power segment EBITDA margin reached 21.4%, up 800 basis points. Management raised 2026 free cash flow guidance to \$16 to \$17 billion from \$11.5 to \$12.5 billion, raised revenue and margin guidance, and doubled the buyback authorization to \$20 billion. Total backlog is \$197 billion and expected to pass \$200 billion this quarter.

The number I care about most is gas turbines. Backlog plus slot reservations rose from 116 to 122 gigawatts, and the company expects at least 130 by year end. A slot reservation is a customer paying to hold a place in a production line that is sold out years ahead. That is the same structure I liked in SK Hynix: capacity committed before it is built, by customers who cannot get it elsewhere.

The market is trading GEV as hyperscaler capex beta. That is only partly right. Utilities and independent power producers are the direct buyers, and they buy turbines because load is growing, whoever ends up consuming the power. The stock fell from \$1,029.21 on October 6 to \$815.00 on October 23, then held between \$836 and \$915 through the print and through Friday's selloff.

## Catalyst

Eaton reports on November 3, which is a read on data center electrical demand. After that, the year-end gas backlog figure, and the fourth quarter call in late January with 2027 guidance.

## Risks

The most likely failure is the AI power basket de-rating further on capex fears after the Alphabet and Amazon prints. That would look like GEV closing below the \$796 to \$815 base on the same days CEG and VRT fall.

Wind is the second risk. The full-year wind loss outlook widened to \$400 to \$500 million from about \$400 million. It is a small segment, but it is where every past disappointment at this company has come from.

The third is that slot reservations fell from 63 to 58 gigawatts as some converted to firm orders. That is the expected direction. If reservations keep shrinking without converting, demand is softer than the headline backlog suggests.

I am also adding a second power name next to CEG. They share a driver, and the book will feel it if the theme breaks.
