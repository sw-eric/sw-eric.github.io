## Position

Long SKHY. Reference \$166.19 pre-market, prior close \$165.67. Fifty-two week range \$124.80 to \$194.80. Intended holding period six to twelve months.

## Thesis

Three claims, in descending order of confidence.

**First, high-bandwidth memory does not behave like commodity memory.** Conventional DRAM is interchangeable, priced on spot, and competed on cost per bit. HBM is sold under multi-year agreements at negotiated prices, qualified customer by customer, and gated by advanced packaging capacity that sits outside the memory maker's own fabs. Winning qualification holds a socket for a product generation. The second quarter print is the evidence: operating profit of ₩60.54 trillion on revenue of ₩79.32 trillion, a 76% operating margin, higher than TSMC's. A commodity producer does not earn a foundry's margin, even at a cycle peak.

**Second, the packaging constraint is easing on a schedule that favors the incumbent.** TSMC disclosed on August 11 that its 5.5 times reticle CoWoS solution entered mass production, accommodating up to twelve HBM4 stacks at yields above 98%. HBM4 volume in 2027 required that capacity to exist at production yields. It now does, and SK Hynix is the primary qualified supplier into it.

**Third, and least certain, supply tightness may widen rather than resolve.** SK Group chairman Chey Tae-won said on August 14 that the company is weighing a joint venture structure for new fabs specifically to share capital expenditure and overcapacity risk, and that tight supply could intensify into 2027. That is a chairman talking his own book, so I discount it. It is nonetheless the clearest signal to date that the supplier itself does not intend to solve the shortage by building unilaterally.

Valuation supports rather than drives the case. The stock trades near 19.5 times trailing earnings against Micron at 20.7. Trailing multiples flatter any company whose earnings are expanding this fast, so I do not lean on this.

## Catalyst

The 2027 HBM4 contract round, negotiating in the second half of this year.

Every existing HBM price reflects agreements struck when supply was the binding constraint. The 2027 round is the first genuine price discovery since the sold-out period began, and it is where I learn whether pricing power survives contact with CXMT capacity and with customers who have absorbed two years of increases. Flat or higher pricing confirms that HBM has escaped commodity economics. Concessions would be the earliest available read on saturation, arriving two to three quarters before it reaches any income statement.

Nothing else on the calendar carries comparable information.

## Risks

**The instrument may not track the thesis.** This is the risk I weight most heavily. On August 6 the ADR fell 10.37% on SanDisk NAND guidance. On August 12 it rose 8% alongside SanDisk, Western Digital, and Micron. On August 13 and 14 it rose again on cooler US inflation data and on SanDisk's capital return announcement. Four observations, none of them Korean fundamentals. If SKHY prices as US memory-complex beta, my Korean sourcing advantage does not reach the price, and my reason for preferring it over Micron is weaker than I assumed when I chose it.

**CXMT.** The July 27 Shanghai listing raised ¥57.92 billion explicitly to fund domestic HBM3 mass production by the end of 2026. Current output is trailing-node commodity and absent from leading-edge HBM. That may hold. It now has capital attached to the question.

**Downstream demand destruction is already visible.** Samsung's mobile division posted the first operating loss in its history in the same quarter its chip division set a record. TrendForce has third-quarter conventional DRAM contract prices decelerating to plus 13 to 18% from 58 to 63%, attributed to buyers being unable to absorb further increases. Alphabet has raised phone prices citing a severe memory crunch. HP reports on August 26 having already guided to a margin trough.

**Consensus is one-directional.** Every analyst initiating coverage since the July listing has been bullish. Unanimity is not evidence of correctness, and it removes the marginal buyer.

**Currency and basis.** Won exposure is unhedged and unhedgeable in this account. The ADR is five weeks old, so its premium or discount to the Seoul line has no history I can reason from.

## Entry

The entry is poor and I am not going to pretend otherwise.

The stock has risen 27.7% from its August 6 close of \$130.17. The KOSPI closed today at a record 6,977.94, its fifth consecutive gain, up 11.5% on the week after seven losing weeks, with roughly \$2 billion of foreign net buying. The proximate causes were a soft US inflation print and a NAND company's capital return announcement. Neither changed anything I believe about SK Hynix.

What I am buying is a thesis about 2027 HBM economics. What I am paying is a price set by six days of momentum. Those are not the same thing, and the gap between them is the cost of having taken six weeks to decide.

## Limits

Position capped at 30% of the account. Given realized volatility, that cap governs concentration rather than risk, and I do not treat it as a risk control.

I am substituting thesis invalidation for the standing 25% price stop, and recording the substitution in advance. This name has moved more than 17% across two sessions on third-party guidance, so a price stop would fire on noise before the thesis resolves.

Invalidation conditions: 2027 HBM4 contracts settling below 2026 levels; CXMT achieving qualified HBM shipment at any leading customer; two consecutive quarters of declining HBM revenue at SK Hynix; conventional DRAM contract prices turning negative quarter over quarter.

Entry executed on a separate day from any other purchase, given T+1 settlement in a cash account.

## What I am not claiming

I am not claiming this is cheap, and I have not built a model. I am not claiming to have found a bottom, because the bottom was six days and 28% ago. I am not claiming an informational edge until the correlation question above is settled with more than four observations.

The claim is narrower. HBM economics differ structurally from DRAM economics, SK Hynix is positioned to capture that difference, and the 2027 contract round will tell me within six months whether I am right.
