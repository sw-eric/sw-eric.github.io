## Position

Long CEG in two legs. Leg one is 0.27 shares at a limit of \$258, about 5% of the account, re-entered daily until the third quarter print. Leg two is another 5% after the print, only if 2026 guidance holds, the growth target through 2029 is reiterated, and the stock is at or above \$247. CEG closed at \$262.96 on October 29. The fifty-two week high is \$399.38 and thirty day realized volatility is 56%. I intend to hold two to nine months.

## Thesis

The market is pricing Constellation as if its new hyperscaler contracts add nothing.

On October 6, Constellation signed a twenty-year agreement with Google that funds 890 megawatts of uprates across eleven nuclear units, plus a fifteen-year deal for 2,700 megawatts of the existing fleet ([POWER](https://www.powermag.com/google-deal-backs-890-mw-of-constellation-nuclear-uprates-at-11-pjm-reactors/)). About a week earlier it signed 690 megawatts with Amazon at Calvert Cliffs ([Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/nuclear/google-constellation-doe-nuclear-plant-investment/)). The stock closed at \$267.62 the day before the Google announcement and \$300.40 the day after. It closed at \$262.96 today, so it has given back the entire move while nothing in the contracts has changed.

Guidance was raised on August 6 to \$11.50 to \$12.50 a share, with base earnings expected to grow more than 20% a year through 2029 ([SentiSense](https://app.sentisense.ai/stories/constellation-energy-expects-2026-adjusted-operating-eps-of-11.50-12.50-08062026)). At the midpoint, the stock trades near 22 times this year's earnings. That is expensive for a utility, but Constellation has not been priced as one for two years, and it is reasonable for that growth with twenty-year contracts behind it.

The uprates are the part I like most. They add capacity at sites that are already licensed and connected to the grid, so they skip most of the interconnection queue. Google is paying for megawatts that would not otherwise exist, which is a different deal from buying existing output off the grid and is the kind regulators have been less hostile to.

CEG also fits next to SKHY because the two lose for different reasons. SKHY is long the hardware hyperscalers buy; CEG is long the power that runs it, on contracts longer than any hardware cycle. Memory fails on pricing and supply, while power fails on regulation and rates.

## Catalyst

Third quarter results in early November. Zacks lists November 6, but I have not confirmed the date with the company. I am watching three lines: whether the guidance range holds, whether the 2029 target is reiterated, and free cash flow.

## Risks

The most likely failure starts tomorrow. Alphabet fell 13% today and Amazon is down 10.5% after hours, and the market trades CEG as part of the AI power basket. That would look like CEG closing under the \$247.20 double low on a day when Alphabet and Vistra are also down. A close under \$240 ends the trade.

Free cash flow was negative in each of the last three reported quarters, and debt reached \$24.7 billion at June 30 after the Calpine acquisition, up from \$8.3 billion a year earlier ([Trefis](https://www.trefis.com/stock/ceg/articles/619625/should-you-buy-the-dip-in-constellation-energy-stock/2026-10-21)). That is the real debate on this stock. If the print shows another negative quarter, the stock can fall with guidance intact.

A final FERC or PJM rule restricting co-location at existing nuclear plants would hurt the existing-fleet deals ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/pjm-data-center-colocation-ferc-vistra-constellation/815941/)). The uprate deal is less exposed because the megawatts are new.

High-multiple names have also sold off with long-term yields this high, and a December hike would hit this stock before it hits the earnings.

If the GOOGL stop fills tomorrow, I am still partly in the Google trade through this one, since Google is now a Constellation customer.
