## The argument

This position exists for a different reason than the other two in the book, and the reason is a divergence rather than a growth rate.

Over the twelve months to early August 2026, Synopsys fell roughly 36% while the iShares Semiconductor ETF returned approximately 106%. A stock that declines while its sector doubles is not being priced on the sector's variable. Whatever is setting the price at \$415.50, it is not hyperscaler capital expenditure, and for a portfolio already long that factor through SKHY and GOOGL, that is the entire point of owning it.

The business underneath the divergence is a toolchain rather than a component. Every advanced chip, whether it is a Nvidia accelerator, an Alphabet Tensor Processing Unit, a Broadcom custom application-specific integrated circuit, or a Chinese design nobody has qualified yet, is laid out in electronic design automation software, and Synopsys and Cadence Design Systems hold that market between them. Revenue arrives as multi-year licenses tied to customer research budgets and design starts, not to how many racks are installed in a given quarter. Synopsys exited fiscal 2025 with backlog above \$11 billion, up from \$10.1 billion the prior quarter. For fiscal 2026 it guides revenue of \$9.625 billion to \$9.705 billion, an operating margin target of 40.5%, roughly 320 basis points above fiscal 2025, and non-GAAP earnings per share of \$14.72 to \$14.80. At \$415.50 against the midpoint of that guidance, the forward multiple is approximately 28 times.

Design activity is the variable that holds up in a capital expenditure pause. If hyperscaler spending decelerates in 2027, inspection tools and optical transceivers see it in orders within two quarters. Chip design programs already underway do not stop, because the sunk cost of an abandoned tapeout exceeds the license fee. That is the structural reason to expect lower correlation, and it is a reason rather than a measurement.

## Catalyst

Third quarter fiscal 2026 results on August 26, 2026, twelve days from writing. The specific item is the Design IP segment, which declined year over year in the second quarter even as the company raised full year guidance on revenue, operating margin, earnings per share, and free cash flow. Management has characterized fiscal 2026 as a transition year for that segment. A second consecutive quarter of Design IP decline against raised guidance elsewhere would tell me the problem is structural rather than transitional. The company has also flagged that it will give more detail on artificial intelligence monetization at an upcoming Investor Day, which is the second dated event worth watching.

Worth noting for scheduling: August 26 is also the date HP reports fiscal third quarter results, which is the demand-destruction checkpoint written into the SKHY memo. Two of the book's three invalidation tests land on the same day.

## Risks

The reason the stock is cheap relative to its sector is that three identifiable things went wrong, and none of them are resolved.

The Ansys acquisition put more than \$10 billion of long-term debt on a balance sheet that historically carried almost none, and the associated intangible amortization of roughly \$504 million annually distorts reported earnings badly enough that the trailing multiple near 81 times is not usable. Synergy targets of \$400 million in annual run-rate revenue have been cited by management, and I have no independent basis for judging whether they arrive.

China represents approximately 10% of revenue and is subject to United States export restrictions on chip design software. Restrictions imposed in late May 2025 were rescinded on July 2 of that year, which demonstrates that the exposure moves on policy decisions I cannot forecast and that reverse without warning in either direction. China revenue excluding Ansys fell 22% in fiscal 2025.

Elliott Investment Management's managing partner joined the board following the second quarter. Activist involvement is evidence that outside pressure was judged necessary, which is a signal about the preceding period as much as the coming one. Nvidia separately took a \$2 billion equity position as part of a strategic partnership. I treat both as facts about who is watching rather than as endorsements of value.

Entry timing is the risk I am least comfortable with. Three days before writing, the stock traded near \$384. It is \$415.50 now, roughly 8% higher, so I am buying a bounce and not a low. The fifty-two week low of \$366.00 was set inside the last thirteen weeks, which means the down-move is recent rather than old and stale.

The decorrelation premise is a hypothesis, not a measurement. It rests on twelve months of relative performance and a structural argument about revenue recognition. I have not calculated a beta to SKHY, and if a broad artificial intelligence repricing arrives, everything in the book will fall together regardless of what the toolchain business does.

## What I am not claiming

I am not claiming to have modeled the Ansys integration. The synergy figures in this memo are management's and I have accepted them without independent work, which is the weakest part of the analysis.

I am not claiming an informational edge. My Korean-language sourcing advantage is worth nothing on a company whose problems are American export policy and an American software acquisition.

I am not claiming this name is uncorrelated with the rest of the book. I am claiming it is less correlated, on a structural argument I have set out and not verified statistically.

The claim is narrower: that electronic design automation revenue tracks design activity rather than capital expenditure, that Synopsys fell 36% while its sector doubled because of company problems rather than sector problems, and that the August 26 print will tell me within two weeks whether the Design IP issue is a transition or a decline.
