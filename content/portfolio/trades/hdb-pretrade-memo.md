## Thesis

India's largest private bank is being priced as broken, and the quarter it just reported says the core business is not.

Second quarter results on October 17 showed net interest income of ₹34,619 crore, up 9.7%, gross advances up 16.3%, and deposits up 18.8% ([Finology](https://ticker.finology.in/discover/market-update/hdfc-bank-q2-fy27-results-analysis)). Net profit grew only 4.7%, to ₹19,510 crore, and the weak lines were treasury income, down 67.4%, and provisions, not the loan book. Gross NPAs improved to 1.11% and credit costs were 44 basis points ([Whalesbook](https://www.whalesbook.com/corporate-news/English/bankingfinance/HDFC-Bank-Q2-FY27-Anup-Bagchi-Appointed-CEO-Margin-Bottom-Signaled/6adb97b9c6c6dccbb9060b1e)).

The real problem is the margin. Net interest margin on total assets fell to 3.16% from 3.26%, the lowest in twelve quarters, as deposit costs rose. Management said this quarter is the low point and expects improvement from the third quarter. About 70% of the loan book floats, so the repricing argument is plausible, but it is still management's claim and not yet a number.

The selling looks more like ownership changing hands than a verdict on the bank. Foreign investors held 39.4% at the end of September, down from 48.4% a year earlier, while domestic mutual funds rose to 31.9% from 26% ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/hdfc-bank-shares-down-30-in-2026-fpi-love-for-indias-largest-bank-wanes-whats-ahead-563074-2026-10-22)). Forty-eight of fifty brokerages rate it a buy, which tells me the bad news is not a secret. I am betting that most of the forced selling is done.

It also has little to do with the rest of the book. Its three-month correlation to GOOGL and SNPS is close to zero, and its exposure is to Indian credit and the rupee, not to anyone's capital expenditure.

## Catalyst

New CEO Anup Bagchi's first strategy outline, undated. Deputy MD Kaizad Bharucha retires in December, and naming a successor would remove one overhang. The third quarter results in mid-January are the real test: the first quarter in which the margin has to turn.

## Risks

The most likely failure is that the margin does not turn. That would look like HDB falling through \$21.20 after the January print, a loss of about 7%, or \$10.

Governance is the second risk. Bloomberg reported that an internal probe found two senior executives had disclosed confidential information, and the bank has not commented. Combined with the CEO change and the Deputy MD's early exit, that is a lot of turnover at the top. Any RBI action tied to the probe would hit the multiple regardless of earnings.

The third is oil and the rupee. India imports most of its oil, so a renewed spike in the Gulf weakens the currency and drags the ADR in dollars, even if the bank does nothing wrong. That would show up as HDB falling alongside the rupee and INDA with no company news.

I also know Indian banks less well than anything else I own. The size reflects that.
