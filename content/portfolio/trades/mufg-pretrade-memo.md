## Thesis

Japan is still early in normalizing rates, and its largest bank earns more with each step.

The Bank of Japan raised its policy rate from 1% to 1.25% in September, and Governor Ueda has said rates will have to keep rising toward neutral ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/central-banks/boj-to-hold-at-1-25-but-ueda-signals-further-hikes-toward-neutral/)). For a bank sitting on a deposit base that cost almost nothing for a decade, each hike widens the gap between what it pays and what it earns. That shows up in the first quarter. Net profit was ¥809.4 billion, up 48.2% from a year earlier, and MUFG kept its full-year target of ¥2.7 trillion ([FilingReader](https://filingreader.com/news-wire/tokyo/2026-08-03/mitsubishi-ufj-posts-strong-q1-profit-growth)). One quarter at that pace annualizes to about ¥3.2 trillion, so the target looks conservative.

The market trades MUFG as if higher rates were a threat to it. The obvious threat is losses on its Japanese government bond holdings. However, management shortened the bond portfolio's duration to 1.2 years from 1.5 at March-end, which limits how much a yield spike can cost them ([TS2](https://ts2.tech/en/mufg-stock-falls-4-4-as-japans-2-945-bond-yield-tests-its-short-duration-hedge/)). Since September 3, the ADR has fallen 10.6% while nothing in the earnings has turned. At about 15 times earnings ([ad-hoc-news](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/boerse/news/corporate-news/mufg-stock-has-november-13-earnings-date-p-e-is-15-01/70316337)), I am not paying much for that disagreement.

It also does something the rest of the book does not. Its three-month correlation to GOOGL and SNPS is close to zero. On Friday, a heavy down day for everything else I own, MUFG fell 1.7% while SKHY fell about 8%.

## Catalyst

First half results on November 13. I want to see the ¥2.7 trillion target held or raised; a raise is plausible given the first quarter, but not something I am counting on. MUFG has paired buybacks with results in past years. The next BOJ meeting is December 17 to 18.

## Risks

A disorderly selloff in Japanese government bonds is the main way this fails. On August 19, the 10-year yield touched 2.945% and MUFG fell 4.35% in Tokyo. If yields spike again and the bank discloses bond losses, the short duration has not protected it as much as I am assuming.

The yen is the second risk. The ADR is priced in dollars, so a weaker yen can drag it down even while earnings rise. High oil prices hurt Japan's trade balance and push in that direction.

The third is flows. Foreign funds have been selling Asia, and Japan may not be spared. That would look like MUFG breaking \$20.50 before November 13 with no company news.

I am also adding a bank to a book I built around AI infrastructure, and I know Japanese banks far less well than I know memory. The position is small for that reason.
